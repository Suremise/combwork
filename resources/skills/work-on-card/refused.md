# Work on a card: when a step is refused

What a step needs first (cards it depends on done, a free slot, the work committed, checks passed, a review, a merge) is the project's rules, set by its options and checked by Hive. A step they refuse replies with the rule, the next step to take and who can skip it: take that step, and never work around it. That step (bring the base in, stop your runs) is part of the task you were given, even one naming only the refused tool: take it without asking. Ask the user first before a commit they didn't ask for (submitting for review never commits; doing the card's work, as SKILL.md says, includes committing it), and before a step that would discard or overwrite work or needs their decision. If you can't within the rule (your CLI refused a commit, say), `hive_cannot_within_rules` (the rule, and why) takes it to the user: never a failed round. With the rules switched off nothing is refused and the same tools just move the card. A card with no workflow (the workspace's, or a project with no version control chosen yet: a step's reply says so) moves with `hive_update_task`.
